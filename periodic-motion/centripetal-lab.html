AQA 7408 · 3.6.1.1 · centripetal acceleration and force
velocity v (tangent)centripetal force F (towards the centre)
The speed is constant but the direction keeps changing, so the velocity changes and the mass accelerates towards the centre. A resultant force (here the tension) must act towards the centre. Cut the string and the force vanishes: the mass moves off along the tangent, not outwards.
F = mv²/r and F = mω²r are the same equation (v = ωr), but they predict opposite trends with r. Ask: what is being kept constant? At constant v a tighter circle needs more force; at constant ω (a turntable) points further out need more force.

a = v²/r = ω²r    F = mv²/r = mω²r

Answers to 3 significant figures. Use g = 9.81 N kg−1.
