AQA 7408 · 3.6.1.2 · x–t, v–t and a–t graphs
v is the gradient of x–t, and a is the gradient of v–t. Each graph is a quarter cycle (π/2) ahead of the one before. The a–t graph is the x–t graph upside down (a = −ω²x), so a and x are in antiphase.
v against x is an ellipse: v = ±ω√(A² − x²), so v = ±ωA at x = 0 and v = 0 at x = ±A. a against x is a straight line through the origin with gradient −ω². The orange dot follows the time cursor on the first tab.
