Circular Motion in Action

AQA 7408 · 3.6.1.1 · applying F = mv²/r
weight mgtension Tfaded ball: moving round the circle
At the top, weight and tension both point down: T + mg = mv²/r. At the bottom, tension points up and weight down: T − mg = mv²/r. The string goes slack at the top if v² < gr, so the minimum speed at the top is √(gr). Speed at the bottom comes from energy: vb² = vt² + 4gr.
At the top of the hump the resultant must be downwards (towards the centre): mg − R = mv²/r, so the reaction R = mg − mv²/r is less than the weight. Passengers feel lighter. When v = √(gr), R = 0 and the car leaves the road.
Resolve the tension (or the normal reaction on a banked track). Vertically: T cos θ = mg (no vertical acceleration). Horizontally: T sin θ = mv²/r. Dividing gives tan θ = v²/(rg), which does not depend on the mass.
