AQA 7408 · Required practical 7 · SHM with a mass–spring system and a pendulum
Stopwatch readings include your reaction time. Take three repeats for each mass and at least five masses.
Release from less than 10°. Time from the centre of the swing using a fiducial marker.
Look at the intercept. The string length l is measured to the top of the bob, but the pendulum length is to its centre of mass. That is a systematic error: it shifts the line but does not change the gradient, so g is still correct.

Method

1. Hang the spring from a clamp; add a mass hanger. Displace the mass a small distance and release.
2. Time 10 oscillations from the equilibrium position (fiducial marker). Repeat 3 times and find the mean; T = mean ÷ 10.
3. Repeat for at least 5 masses. Plot T² against m: gradient = 4π²/k, so k = 4π²/gradient.
4. Pendulum: vary l from about 0.20 m to 1.20 m. Small amplitude (under 10°). Plot T² against l: gradient = 4π²/g, so g = 4π²/gradient.

Reducing uncertainty

Timing many oscillations divides the reaction-time uncertainty by 10.
A fiducial marker at the centre: the bob moves fastest there, so the instant it passes is sharpest.
Measure l with a metre rule at eye level (no parallax), to the centre of the bob.

CPAC and safety

CPAC 1 follow the written method; CPAC 2 choose 10 oscillations and a fiducial marker; CPAC 4 record raw times to 0.01 s in a table with units; CPAC 5 plot T² against m (or l) and calculate k (or g) from the gradient.
Risk: masses can fall if the spring is overloaded or the clamp slips: clamp the stand to the bench (or use a G-clamp/counterweight), keep feet clear, and do not exceed the spring's elastic limit. Wear eye protection when springs are stretched.
Why plot T² and not T? T = 2π√(m/k) gives T² = (4π²/k)m. Plotting T² against m gives a straight line whose gradient leads straight to k.
