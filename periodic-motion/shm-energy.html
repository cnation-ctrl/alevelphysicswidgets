AQA 7408 · 3.6.1.3 · energy in SHM and damping
kinetic Ekpotential Eptotal
With no damping the total energy is constant: E = ½kA² = ½mω²A². Potential energy Ep = ½kx² is a parabola, and Ek = ½k(A² − x²) is an upside-down parabola. They cross at x = ±A/√2 ≈ ±0.71A, not at A/2.
Ek and Ep each go from zero to maximum twice every cycle, so energy oscillates at twice the frequency of the displacement (period T/2). Neither ever goes negative. They are always in antiphase and add to a constant.
Damping is a force opposing the motion (friction, air resistance) that transfers energy out of the oscillator, usually as heat. Light: the amplitude decays gradually and T stays almost the same. Critical: returns to equilibrium in the shortest time with no overshoot (car suspension). Heavy: no oscillation, slow return.
