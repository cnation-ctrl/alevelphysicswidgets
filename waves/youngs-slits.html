Young's Double-Slit Lab

AQA 7408 · 3.3.2.1 · Young's double-slit experiment, w = λD/s
w = λD / s

An unknown laser shines through slits with s = mm. The screen is D = m away. Use the ruler to measure across several fringes, then calculate λ.

Measure from the centre of one bright fringe to the centre of another, across as many fringe spacings as possible, then divide. This reduces the percentage uncertainty in w.

Using a laser safely (class 2 laser, ≤ 1 mW)

Never look directly into the beam or at a reflection of it (it can damage the retina).
Never point the laser at anyone; keep the beam below eye level, horizontal.
Remove shiny objects (watches, rings) and avoid reflective surfaces in the beam path.
Use a matt screen; display a warning sign; switch off when not measuring.
Turn on the room lights between readings so pupils are not dilated.
Why a laser? It is monochromatic (one wavelength, so sharp fringes), coherent (constant phase relationship), and intense, so fringes are bright enough to measure. Young (1801) used a single slit in front of sunlight to make the light reaching the two slits coherent.
