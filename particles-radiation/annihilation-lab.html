Annihilation and Pair Production

AQA 7408 · 3.2.1.3 · particles, antiparticles and photons
Why two photons? The total momentum before is (about) zero, so the photons must go off in opposite directions with equal energy. One photon alone would carry momentum. This is how a PET scanner locates a positron-emitting tracer: the detectors look for two 0.511 MeV photons arriving at the same time on opposite sides.

Which pairs can this photon make?

Pair production needs hf ≥ 2E0, where E0 is the rest energy of one particle. It happens near a nucleus, which takes a little momentum. Any extra energy becomes kinetic energy of the pair.
